== notation
range: $[n] = \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$
xor: $\oplus$ is bitwise exclusive or
gray: $g(n) = n \oplus \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ is the Gray code
graydec: $\bar{g}(m) = \bigoplus_{k=0}^{7} \lfloor m/2^k \rfloor$ is the 8-bit Gray decoder
ham: $d(a, b)$ is the number of bits in which $a$ and $b$ differ
pow2: $2^{\mathbb{N}} = \{1, 2, 4, 8, \ldots\}$, so $a \oplus b \in 2^{\mathbb{N}}$ says that $a$ and $b$ differ in exactly one bit
nuc: $\mathcal{N} = \{A, C, G, T\}$, with codes $e(A) = 0$, $e(C) = 1$, $e(G) = 3$, $e(T) = 2$ (binary 00, 01, 11, 10)
rc: $r(x)$ and $c(x)$ are the row and column of letter $x$ on the 2 × 2 map: $r(A) = r(C) = 0$, $r(G) = r(T) = 1$, $c(A) = c(T) = 0$, $c(C) = c(G) = 1$
kmer: $E_k(x_1 \cdots x_k) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} 4^{\,k-i}\, e(x_i)$ is the cell of a $k$-mer; $R(xy) = e(x)$ and $C(xy) = e(y)$ are the row and column of a dinucleotide
aa: $\mathcal{A}$ is the 20 amino acids in group order A V L I F Y W M C P G S T N Q D E H K R; $e(a) \in [20]$ is the position of $a$ in that order and $\gamma(a) = g(e(a))$ its 5-bit code
delta: $\delta(a, b) = d(\gamma(a), \gamma(b))$ is the distance between the codes of amino acids $a$ and $b$
rho: $\rho(a) = a$ is the raw group-order index of amino acid number $a \in [20]$
c8: $C_8$ is the 8-residue example map: $C_8(i, j) = 1$ for $(i, j) = (0,3), (1,4), (2,5), (0,5)$ and their mirror images, $0$ otherwise
cij: $c_{ij} = 8\,g(i) + g(j)$ is the Gray-indexed cell of the residue pair $(i, j)$
P: $P = \{c_{03}, c_{14}, c_{25}, c_{05}\}$ is the set of cells holding the example's contacts
Nk: $N_k = \#\{(a, b) \in [20]^2 \mid a \ne b,\ d(g(a), g(b)) = k\}$ counts the ordered pairs of amino-acid codes at distance $k$
sigma: $\sigma(s)$ is the separation bin of residues $s$ apart: $0$ for $s \le 10$, $1$ for $11 \le s \le 20$, $2$ for $21 \le s \le 30$, $3$ for $s \ge 31$
cube: $Q_w(v, m)$ is the set of $c \in [2^w]$ that agree with $v$ on every bit not set in $m$: the cube with fixed bits $v$ and free bits $m$
toy: $T$ is a worked table on two bits, $T(0) = T(1) = 1$ and $T(2) = T(3) = 0$, covered by the single cube $Q_2(0, 1)$
div: $2^u \mid v$ says that $v$ is a multiple of $2^u$
